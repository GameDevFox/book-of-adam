this repo (e.g. `packages/memorial/data/texts/`) it is quoted from there;
otherwise the source is named in that entry's notes.

Entries are plain text — no Markdown, so they paste cleanly anywhere.

## 1. Brigham Young — novels are without profit (JD 19:64)

*Source: `packages/memorial/data/texts/` — Journal of Discourses, jod.mrm.org transcription.*

### Markdown

> Study the Bible, the Book of Mormon, the Doctrine and Covenants, read the sermons that are published in the Deseret News, as well as all the standard works of the Church. Such reading will afford you instruction and improvement; but novels allure the mind and are without profit.

— Brigham Young, "Items of History—The Pioneers—Talking to the Children—Peace in Utah—God a Personage of Tabernacle—The Foolish Fashions," *Journal of Discourses* 19:64 (July 24, 1877), reported by Geo. F. Gibbs. https://jod.mrm.org/19/60/

"Study the Bible, the Book of Mormon, the Doctrine and Covenants, read the

works of the Church. Such reading will afford you instruction and improvement;

-- Brigham Young, "Items of History...Talking to the Children...", Journal of
   Discourses 19:64 (July 24, 1877), reported by Geo. F. Gibbs.
   https://jod.mrm.org/19/60/

## 2. Brigham Young — dress and study (JD 19:64–65)

*Source: `packages/memorial/data/texts/` — Journal of Discourses, jod.mrm.org transcription.*

### Markdown

> Little girls, permit me to ask you, Won't you be so kind and so good as to take those pins or the india-rubber cords out of the back of the skirts of your dresses, so that you will look comely. They make you look uncomely, to see your dresses drawn around you, showing your form. Mothers ought to be ashamed of teaching their children such things. Dress your children and yourselves in that comely, angelic manner that, were an angel to visit you, you would not feel ashamed. I am very pleased to say that there are some of our girls, and numbered among them are some of my own, whom you could not get to adopt these follies. Ask your mothers, then, to make your clothes suitable and becoming; and keep your hair smooth and nice. The hair is given to the female for adornment; and therefore let the ladies, young and old, adorn their heads with their hair. Mothers should study and children should study to preserve the skin of the children from being ruined by dirt, and the heat of a scorching sun, and to keep themselves clean and pure; but children, now remember, study those books that teach you the way of life and salvation.

— Brigham Young, "Items of History—The Pioneers—Talking to the Children—Peace in Utah—God a Personage of Tabernacle—The Foolish Fashions," *Journal of Discourses* 19:64–65 (July 24, 1877), reported by Geo. F. Gibbs. https://jod.mrm.org/19/60/

## 3. Brigham Young — "the Bible is high treason" (JD 19:61)

*Source: `packages/memorial/data/texts/` — Journal of Discourses, jod.mrm.org transcription.*

"When Joseph had his trial, the great accusation against him was that he believed in the fulfillment of prophecy—the prophecies that had been made by Prophets of old and contained in Holy Writ. When Judge King asked Joseph if he believed the predictions of Daniel the Prophet, that in the latter days the God of heaven would set up a kingdom which should succeed and finally rule and hold dominion over all other kingdoms, Joseph replied that he did believe this scripture as well as the rest. This was considered treason! Joseph's lawyer turned to Judge King and said, “Judge, I think you had better write it down that the Bible is high treason,” and this was all they found against him."

— Brigham Young, "Items of History—The Pioneers—Talking to the Children—Peace in Utah—God a Personage of Tabernacle—The Foolish Fashions," Journal of Discourses 19:61 (July 24, 1877), reported by Geo. F. Gibbs. https://jod.mrm.org/19/60/

Short cite: — Brigham Young, JD 19:61 (July 24, 1877)

### Notes

- Reads "When Joseph HAD his trial" — not "has".
- Wholly on p. 61 (verified against cached source HTML), but excerpted from the
  middle of a long paragraph. Starts and ends on sentence boundaries, so no
  ellipses are strictly required; add a trailing one if your style wants the
  mid-paragraph cut marked.
- Same address as entries 1 and 2: Sunday School children, New Tabernacle,
  July 24, 1877.
- CONTESTED — see the caution recorded with this entry before sharing. This is
  a recollection given ~39 years after the events, describing the November 1838
  Richmond hearing before Judge Austin A. King. That proceeding was a
  preliminary examination, not a trial, and it did not end with nothing found:
  Joseph Smith was held over on charges including treason and committed to
  Liberty Jail, leaving custody in April 1839. Verify against a source on the
  hearing before relying on "all they found against him".

## 4. Brigham Young — "this is a spiritual wife" (HC 6:322, not in the JoD)

*Source: History of the Church 6:322, BYU Studies online edition (https://byustudies.byu.edu/online-book/history-of-the-church-volume-6/volume-6-chapter-15), retrieved 2026-10-02. NOT in packages/memorial/data/texts/ — the Journal of Discourses begins in 1851; this is 1844.*

"I want a wife that can take care of my children when I am away, who can pray, lay on hands, anoint with oil, and baffle the enemy; and this is a spiritual wife."

— Brigham Young, remarks at the general conference, Nauvoo, Illinois, morning session, April 9, 1844, History of the Church 6:322. https://byustudies.byu.edu/online-book/history-of-the-church-volume-6/volume-6-chapter-15

Short cite: — Brigham Young, HC 6:322 (April 9, 1844)

### Notes

- Searched for in the Journal of Discourses first (exact and fuzzy, all 26
  volumes): not there, nor anything like it. "Spiritual wife" occurs only twice
  in the JoD (JD 1:109, JD 3:125), both about the "spiritual wife doctrine" label.
- Wording above is the History of the Church text, which is a later edited
  compilation of the clerks' minutes, not a verbatim report. The Brigham Young
  Center transcript (brighamyoungcenter.org/s/byp/item/720) is sourced "HC
  6:321-322" and reads identically.
- The manuscript minutes read differently. Stapley & Wright, "Female Ritual
  Healing in Mormonism," Journal of Mormon History 37 (Winter 2011), quote them
  as: I want a wife that can take care of my chi[ldre]n when I am away who can
  pray lay on hands anoint with oil & baffle the enemy. Every secondary quotation
  of that manuscript reading seen so far stops there; whether the manuscript also
  carries "and this is a spiritual wife" could not be checked (the article PDF
  was not reachable). Say "History of the Church" when quoting the full sentence.
- Wilford Woodruff's journal for April 9, 1844 records Young's morning remarks
  (preach repentance, the "sweepstakes," electioneering) but not this sentence.
- Context: the preceding sentence is "There is no need of breaking the law of
  the land if you keep the law of the Lord." In Nauvoo in April 1844 "spiritual
  wife" was the Bennett-scandal term; Young is turning it. Readers who look it up
  will see the plural-marriage subtext, so do not present it as only about healing.
- Preceded on the same page by the "Priesthood is fitted to every capacity" and
  "making gods and devils" passages, which readers may want alongside it.
